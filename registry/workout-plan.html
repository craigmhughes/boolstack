Recovery Day for Pressing Muscles

No heavy benching today. Elbow joint warm-up: 2×15 with very light DBs before loading. This protects tendons that curl work stresses.

Weight10–12.5 kg DBs
Rest90 sec

Recommended pick — brachialis emphasis, better carryover to pressing than standard curl. Builds the "thickness" under the bicep.

Sets × Reps3 × 10
Weight8–10 kg DBs
Rest90 sec

Alternative pick — longer stretch on bicep. Choose this OR hammer curl, not both. Running both curl variations on Day B adds unnecessary elbow flexor fatigue that competes with Day C bench performance.

Tricep Assistance — Primary Bench Limiter Fix
DB Floor Press Tricep Strength
Sets × Reps4 × 8
Start Weight16–20 kg each
Rest2 min
Progress+2 kg total

Triceps are the most common bench limiter. Floor press trains lockout specifically. Safer without a rack. Don't anchor to the low suggested weights — if you're benching 60+ kg, you can likely floor press 20–24 kg per hand within a few sessions. Progress by feel.

Progression Rules
When to Add Weight
Lift Condition to Progress Increment
Bench Press (Day A) All 5×5 reps completed cleanly +2.5 kg
Bench Press (Day C) All 4×4 reps with good bar speed +2.5 kg
Underhand Row Top reps achieved with controlled form +5 kg
Barbell Curl All sets clean, no body swing +2.5 kg
DB Floor Press Comfortable completion, no sticking +2 kg total
DB Curls (Day B) Comfortable completion +2 kg per DB
Expected Timeline
6 weeks Noticeable bar speed increase. Setup feels more automatic. Row weight beginning to climb.
3 months +5–10 kg on bench realistic. Floor press approaching bench weight. Curl progressing alongside.
6–9 months +15–25 kg on bench achievable with consistent training, adequate sleep, and sufficient caloric intake. Row should be approaching 80% of bench load.
Row-to-Bench Ratio Target

Long-term: Underhand Row ≈ 80–100% of Bench Press weight. If bench = 60 kg and rows are still 40 kg, bench progress will stall. The upper back is the bench's foundation.

Warm-Up Sets

Always required before bench and floor press. Empty bar → 40% → 60% → 70% single → work sets. Cold shoulder joints under heavy load is how injuries happen. Non-negotiable.

Row Style Matters

Hip hinge, controlled pulling. The row-to-bench ratio only holds if rows are done strictly. Cheat rows with leg drive at 100% bench weight provide different stimulus than controlled 80% rows.

Day B Curl Selection

Pick one curl variation on Day B — not both. Two curl variations plus floor press adds elbow flexor fatigue that bleeds into Day C bench performance. Hammer curl is the recommended pick.

Floor Press Weights

Suggested starting weights (16–20 kg) are conservative. If you bench 60+ kg, you may be ready for 22–26 kg per hand within weeks. Progress by feel, not the suggested ceiling.

⚠ Timeline Caveat

The +15–25 kg projection over 6–9 months assumes near-perfect consistency, adequate sleep, and sufficient food intake. These are not mentioned in most programs but are equally responsible for results as the training itself. Under-eating or chronic poor sleep will stall progress regardless of programming quality.

Why This Works
1 — Triceps → Floor Press

The triceps are responsible for lockout — the most common sticking point in a bench press. Floor press trains this directly without needing a spotter or rack.

2 — Upper Back → Underhand Row

A weak upper back causes bar instability, poor bar path, and energy leakage. Rows fix this and add bicep training as a free bonus.

3 — Elbow Flexors → Barbell Curl

Elbow flexor strength improves bar control during the descent phase of bench. Weak biceps = unstable lowering = energy wasted before the press even begins.

4 — Heavy Practice → Two Bench Days

Strength is a skill. Two exposures per week — one volume focused (5×5) and one intensity focused (4×4) — builds both volume adaptation and heavy load tolerance.
